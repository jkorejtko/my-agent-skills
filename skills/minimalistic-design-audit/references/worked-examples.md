# Worked examples

Use the same test on the relevant viewport and decision area. Assess both where the eye goes and whether a person can still do what they came to do.

## Category filters

A mobile drawer has two filled buttons, **Apply filters** and **Clear filters**. A bright promotion is visible underneath the drawer. The user wants to browse a narrower set of results. Classify the applicable drawer state as **competing**. Make Apply dominant and Clear quieter but visible, preserve the selected values, and stop background promotions from competing with the open drawer. Retest applying, clearing, and recovering from a no-results state. If filters apply automatically, check when results update and what closing the drawer does before proposing to remove Apply.

## Long landing page

A sticky **Book a demo** and the same filled button in the hero remain visible together on mobile. Check whether one surface can carry that action while both would otherwise be visible. Keep a later instance if it serves readers after a distinct decision point. Retest that the action is clear in the first viewport and still available after the reader gets the information needed to decide.

## Information and neutral choices

On a help article, the answer and its heading can be the focus. Keep related links discoverable without inventing a primary CTA. In a consent dialog, preserve equal access to accept and reject optional tracking. Simplify repeated copy or decorative weight without turning the choice into a visual default.

## Example finding

**Mobile category, 390 px, filters open.** Apply and Clear use the same filled style and have equal prominence. Keep Clear visible with a secondary style. Retest that Apply is the only dominant action in the drawer, that Clear still resets the selected filters, and that both controls can be reached and activated by keyboard and touch.
